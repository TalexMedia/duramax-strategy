02 The Equity You've Built

Three Strengths Already In Place

All three are earned, all three are working. Any new video would build on what's already here.

Product equity. US Polymers has been making vinyl building products in Los Angeles since 1983, as part of the Harwal Group, and sells them online under the Duramax brand. The line spans fencing, sheds, decks, patio covers, garages, and the PVC wall panels, all backed by a fully transferable lifetime warranty. This is a real product with four decades behind it, so the product is not the question. The question is how many of the right people find it and buy.

Story equity. The brand has a real point of view, not just a spec sheet. The products are made in Los Angeles, they're 100% recyclable, and the company recycles over a million pounds of poly vinyl a year, which is why it can call itself "a socially responsible company." On the About page it even names the feeling it's selling, how a finished yard makes the owner feel. There's a lot here that a buyer would respond to, and almost none of it is on video.

Channel equity. The web properties and the traffic are already there. The division runs the Duramax sites, the online store, a YouTube channel with nearly 80 videos, an active Instagram, and a Facebook page. The places to publish and the systems to sell are in place, so the missing piece is the video that earns new visitors and convinces the ones you already have.

What none of it does yet is bring in a new buyer. Every video explains how to assemble a product someone already owns, so it does nothing for discovery, leads, or conversion, which is the whole job of the Internet Division. The fix is video that does three things: earn search traffic, give the product pages social proof, and bring new buyers in. The three ideas below are where we'd start.

03 Lead With Value

The Legendeering Framework, In One Page

The methodology behind every Talex production. Five directives, one core principle.

Legendeering is Talex's proprietary strategy. The core principle is straightforward: lead with value, always. The brand earns trust by giving without expectation, and that trust grows over time because people keep choosing to watch.

Five directives drive it:

  • ·Give Without Expectation. Value delivered without strings. The kind of generosity that builds loyalty money cannot buy.
  • ·Be Relentlessly Authentic. Say true things. Show real work. Speak like a human being.
  • ·It's Not About You. It's About Them. The audience has to come first. It's all about their wants and needs and making something they will choose to watch.
  • ·Start With Story, Follow With Facts. Story gives information shape, context, and emotional weight. Facts alone rarely move people.
  • ·Consistency Is Key. Trust is built in moments, repeated over time.

04 Three Concepts

Three Video Concepts Built To Grow The Numbers

Each one is built to do a job your division is measured on: more traffic, more leads, more online sales.

01
"Before You Buy"
A returning series where each episode answers one real question buyers ask before they choose: vinyl versus FRP versus drywall for a wet room, how long a vinyl fence actually lasts, how to pick a shed that survives your weather. A US Polymers expert answers in plain language, on camera, with real examples. These are the exact questions people type into Google and YouTube, so each episode is built to be found by someone who is already shopping. That is new traffic and new leads every month, and a library that keeps working long after it's posted. It takes the instinct behind the install videos, which is to be useful, and points it at the buyer who hasn't purchased yet.
02
"The Backyard Project"
A returning series where each episode follows one homeowner through a real backyard transformation, from a bare or tired yard to a finished space with a new fence, a deck, a patio cover, or a shed. The homeowner is the on-camera protagonist, and a Duramax installer or expert appears as the guide. Each episode is a different family and a different yard, shot the same way, so people come back for the next reveal. The films do real work for the online business: cut down to short clips, they run as paid social and pre-roll, and the full versions sit on the product pages as the social proof that turns a browser into a buyer.
03
"The Build-Out"
A returning series where each episode follows one business owner opening or renovating a space that has to stay clean and survive hard use: a restaurant kitchen, a car wash, a brewery, a dairy, a food-processing room. The owner is the protagonist, the build is the story, and the Duramax panels are part of how they pull it off on time. Each episode is a different business and a different industry, in the same format. It turns the six written testimonials on the panels site into real people on camera, which is the highest-converting kind of proof for a high-ticket buyer, and it gives the sales team a film to send the next prospect.
